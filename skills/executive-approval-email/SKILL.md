## What You'll Receive

A professional executive approval email including:

1. **Clear Subject Line**: Purpose and action needed
2. **Executive Summary**: Deal overview in 3-4 sentences
3. **Key Terms Table**: Financial, term, and critical provisions
4. **Risk Summary**: Balanced view of risks and mitigations
5. **Business Justification**: Why this deal makes sense
6. **Recommendation & Next Steps**: Clear ask with deadline

## Drafting Instructions

**Generate a concise, executive-ready approval email following these guidelines:**

### 1. Subject Line Format
Use this structure: [ACTION REQUIRED] [Deal Type] Approval: [Counterparty] - [$ Value] - Signature by [Date]

Example: "ACTION REQUIRED: Vendor Agreement Approval: Acme Corp - $500K - Signature by Dec 15"

### 2. Opening Paragraph (2-3 sentences)
State bottom line:
- What you're asking for (contract approval)
- Who the counterparty is
- Total value and term
- Why (business purpose in one sentence)

Example: "I'm requesting your approval to execute a vendor services agreement with Acme Corp for $500,000 over a 2-year term. Acme will provide [service] which will enable us to [business benefit]. The contract has been fully negotiated and is ready for signature."

### 3. Deal Overview Table
Present key information in scannable table format:

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Counterparty** | [Name and what they do] |
| **Services/Product** | [What we're getting] |
| **Total Contract Value** | [$ amount over term] |
| **Payment Terms** | [e.g., $25K/month, Net 30] |
| **Term** | [Duration] |
| **Start Date** | [Date] |
| **Termination** | [Notice period and any penalties] |
| **Key Contacts** | [Their side and our side] |

### 4. Business Justification (3-5 sentences)
Explain why this makes sense:
- Business problem this solves or opportunity it enables
- Strategic alignment with company goals
- Why now (timing/urgency)
- Why this vendor/partner (if applicable)
- Expected ROI or business impact

Focus on business value, not just features.

### 5. Key Terms Highlights
Summarize 4-6 most important contractual terms in bullet format:

**Financial Terms:**
- [Key pricing point or protection]
- [Payment terms or pricing caps]

**Legal Terms:**
- [Key liability protection or cap]
- [Important IP or data rights]
- [Notable termination or flexibility rights]

Only highlight terms that matter to executive decision-making.

### 6. Risk Summary & Mitigations
Present balanced view:

**Key Risks:**
1. **[Risk]**: [Brief description] — **Mitigation**: [How we've addressed it]
2. **[Risk]**: [Brief description] — **Mitigation**: [How we've addressed it]
3. Continue for 2-4 key risks...

If there are minimal risks, state: "This is a low-risk, standard [agreement type] with market-standard terms."

### 7. Negotiation Summary (if applicable)
If significant negotiation occurred, briefly highlight improvements in bullet format:
- [Change 1 - e.g., "Reduced liability cap from $5M to $2M"]
- [Change 2 - e.g., "Added 30-day termination for convenience"]
- [Change 3]

Skip this section if minimal negotiation or renewal.

### 8. Alternatives Considered (if applicable)
If relevant: "We evaluated [X] alternatives and selected [Counterparty] because [key differentiator/advantage]."

Only include if there was a meaningful selection process.

### 9. Recommendation & Next Steps
Close with clear ask:

**Recommendation**: I recommend approving this agreement.

**Next Steps:**
- Your approval by: [Date]
- Signature by: [Date]
- Contract start date: [Date]

Please reply with your approval or let me know if you have any questions. The contract is attached for your review.

### 10. Signature
Close with:
- Your name
- Your title
- Date
- Note about attachment

**Output Guidelines:**
- Keep total email to 1-2 pages (executives are busy)
- Lead with bottom line (what you're asking for)
- Use tables and bullets for scannability
- Focus on business impact, not legal details
- Be balanced: acknowledge risks but show mitigations
- Make the ask crystal clear with specific deadline
- Professional but not overly formal tone
- Provide enough detail for decision, not every provision
- Attach the actual contract for reference
- If complex deal, offer to discuss in person/call
